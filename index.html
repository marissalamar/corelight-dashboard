Executive Summary
What the data shows
→ 81 active customers out of 95 ever won. 12.6% churn rate. 2 flagged at risk.
→ 6.6% POV win rate on decided evaluations (5 won, 71 lost). Large in-progress pipeline.
→ 48% of customers are sub-$50K ARR. Revenue is concentrated in a small number of large accounts.
→ 46% of active customers had zero Pendo activity in the last 30 days. Dark accounts or seasonal pattern — unclear.
Key conclusions
01 For customers: more engagement means less churn. Active customers lead churned across all four dimensions — events, minutes, active days, and unique visitors. Two outliers removed from churned pool (1M+ events, 45 visitors) which were skewing averages in a dataset of only 12.
02 For trials: volume doesn't predict conversion. Breadth does. Lost prospects had more events, minutes, and active days. Won POVs had more unique visitors (7 avg vs 5). Internal buy-in matters more than usage volume.
* POV Won sample = 5. Conclusions 01 and 02 are directional — not statistically significant. Validate as pipeline grows.

INTERPRETATION
For trials: volume doesn't predict conversion. Breadth does.
Lost prospects clicked more, spent more time, stayed longer. Raw engagement is not a win signal.
More stakeholders in the trial, more likely to win.
Won POVs had more unique visitors (7 avg vs 5). The only metric where Won leads — broader internal buy-in matters more than raw usage volume.
For customers: more engagement = less churn. Low 30-day activity in active accounts (44%) is worth watching as an early churn signal.
Reasons live in Salesforce opportunity notes and Zoom debrief transcripts. That's the data gap this architecture addresses.
